Privacy Policy

Effective July 17, 2026. Last updated November 11, 2026.

The Short Version

10b10t does not want your data. There is no registration, no email address, no analytics tracking, and nothing is ever sold or shared with advertisers. What little the server touches is listed below.

IP Addresses

10b10t keeps no persistent IP logs. Your IP is not written to disk during normal play, and nothing is retained after you disconnect.

While you are connected, your IP necessarily passes through the services that need it to function:

  • The authentication server, which handles offline-mode logins.
  • Anti-bot protection, which filters join floods and bot attacks.
  • The DDoS protection nodes that proxy your connection to the server.

These services use your IP live to do their job and drop it once your session ends. There is one exception, described below.

The website works the same way. Your IP is used only in memory where it is needed, such as limiting login attempts on the tickets page, and is never written to the website's logs.

What Is Stored

  • Your username and UUID.
  • Your auth password, stored hashed, never in plain text. The server's security is taken extremely seriously; still, as with any service, do not reuse a password you use anywhere else.
  • Normal Minecraft world data: inventory, position, stats, and builds.
  • Playtime, which gates dupes and other features.
  • Chat logs.

Chat Is Public

In-game chat is logged and streamed live on this website for anyone to read. The online player list, with usernames and skin heads, is also shown on the website. If you do not want something public, do not type it in chat.

Website Login and Tickets

If you open a ticket on the tickets page, 10b10t stores:

  • What you write in the ticket, and any replies.
  • Your username and UUID, so the ticket belongs to your account.
  • The category you picked and when each message was sent.

Tickets are private. Only you, while logged in, and the server owner can read them. Closed tickets are deleted 90 days after they are closed.

You log in with a one-time code from /support in game, on the tickets page or with Log in on the main menu. There is no website account, password, or email address. Logging in sets one cookie in your browser that keeps you logged in for up to 24 hours or until you log out. It is not used for anything else.

When you get a code, the server also sends your skin and a few of your game stats (playtime, first join date, deaths, kills, and time since your last death) so the menu can show them to you. Only you can see them. They are held in memory with your login, never saved to disk, and gone when you log out or the login expires. The website fetches your skin from Mojang's skin server itself, so your browser does not contact Mojang for it.

Password Reset

If you set up password reset in the account menu and confirm it in game, the login server stores an authenticator secret for your account. It is used only to check the codes from your app when you reset your password on the website. Turning reset off with /webreset off deletes it.

When you reset your password, the website passes your username, the code and your new password to the login server. The website keeps none of them: they are held in memory until the login server collects them, at most 2 minutes, and are never saved or logged. The login server stores your new password hashed, the same as a password set in game.

Third-Party Services

Some things are handled by third parties with their own privacy policies:

  • The website is served through Cloudflare, which protects it from attacks. Cloudflare handles your connection to the website, including your IP address, under its own privacy policy.
  • Donations go through the shop site. Payment details are handled by the payment processor there; 10b10t never sees your card details.
  • The website loads its font from cdnfonts.com and player skin heads from crafatar.com and mc-heads.net, so your browser makes requests to those services when you visit.
  • The Discord server and YouTube channel are governed by Discord's and Google's own policies.

The One Exception

As stated in the Terms of Service, CSAM is the one case where this is set aside. If it is found, whatever connection data is visible during that active session is captured and preserved on the spot, along with any other available evidence, and included in the report to the authorities. This is a live capture from an ongoing session, not a stored log being pulled up. Outside this one case, there is no IP record to preserve in the first place.
